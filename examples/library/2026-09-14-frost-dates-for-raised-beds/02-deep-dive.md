# Frost dates for raised beds: in depth

> An invented example from Louise's demo library. The sources are made up and live on example.org.

## Soil temperature by crop

| Crop | Minimum soil temperature to plant out | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Tomatoes | 13 °C | [^5] |
| Beans | 12 °C | [^5] |
| Squash and courgettes | 15 °C | [^6] |
| Aubergines | 16 °C | [^6] |

Readings are taken at 10 cm deep, in the morning, when the soil is at its coolest [^5].

## Protection and how much it buys

- A single layer of garden fleece keeps the air under it about 2 °C warmer on a still night [^4].
- A clear plastic cloche over a single plant adds about 3 °C and also warms the soil by day [^7].
- A cold frame over a whole bed adds 4 to 5 °C and lets hardened-off plants go out up to two weeks earlier [^7].

## A worked example for Alex's beds

The example regional table gives Alex's area an average last frost of 2 May and a 10 percent date of 16 May [^2].
With raised beds warming about a week ahead of open ground [^3], tomatoes can usually go out in the week of
9 May, under fleece, once the beds read 13 °C three mornings running [^5]. Squash waits until the beds reach
15 °C, typically late May in the example records [^6].

## What the sources do not settle

The trials behind the "7 to 10 days earlier" figure used dark soil in sheltered gardens [^3]. A pale, windy site
may gain less, so the soil thermometer stays the deciding test [^5] [^8].
