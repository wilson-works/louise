# Sourdough starter care: overview

> An invented example from Louise's demo library. The sources are made up and live on example.org.

## Feeding

At room temperature (about 21 °C) a starter fed once a day at 1 part starter, 1 part flour and 1 part water by
weight peaks in 4 to 8 hours [^1]. A stiffer feed (1:2:2) slows it down and suits a busy week [^1].

For a once-a-week baker, the example guides agree on the fridge: feed, leave it out for an hour or two, then
refrigerate, and feed again once a week [^2].

## Before baking day

Take the starter out the evening before (Friday, for Alex), feed it 1:2:2, and use it when it has roughly doubled
and smells pleasantly sour, usually 8 to 12 hours later [^2] [^3].

## Reading the signs

| What you see | What it means | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Bubbles all through, domed top, doubles after a feed | Healthy and active | [^3] |
| Grey liquid on top ("hooch") | Hungry, not spoiled: pour it off and feed | [^4] |
| Pink, orange or fuzzy patches | Spoiled: throw it out and start again | [^4] |

## Reviving a neglected starter

A starter left in the fridge for a month or more usually recovers with two or three feeds, 12 hours apart, at room
temperature [^5]. If it has not doubled after three feeds, a warmer spot (about 26 °C) often helps [^5].

## In short

Fridge all week, one feed a week, an overnight feed before baking day, and pour off the grey liquid without
worrying about it [^2] [^4].
