# Small-shop bookkeeping software compared: overview

> An invented example from Louise's demo library. The sources are made up and live on example.org.

## The three kinds of tool

| | Spreadsheet template | Desktop software | Cloud subscription |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cost over three years | Free to low [^1] | One licence, plus paid upgrades [^2] | A monthly fee, the highest total [^2] |
| Bank and card imports | By hand, from downloaded files [^1] | Some, by file import [^3] | Automatic feeds from most banks [^3] |
| Sales tax and invoices | Only what you build [^1] | Built in [^2] | Built in [^2] |
| Stock | Only what you build [^4] | Often an add-on [^4] | Usually built in on mid-priced plans [^4] |
| Moving away later | Easy: it is your file [^5] | Exports, with some effort [^5] | Exports vary; check before signing up [^5] |

## What the example survey of small shops found

Shops with fewer than about 20 sales a week mostly stayed happy with a spreadsheet [^6]. Above about 30 sales a
week, the owners in the survey said automatic bank feeds saved them two to three hours a month [^6].

## A recommendation by shop size

- Under 20 sales a week: a good spreadsheet template, reconciled monthly [^1] [^6].
- 20 to 50 sales a week (Alex, at about 40): a cloud subscription, for the bank feeds and invoices [^3] [^6].
- Several staff or a lot of stock: compare desktop and cloud options on stock handling first [^4].

## Before choosing

Check that the tax rules where the shop trades are supported, and export a test file on day one so moving later
is never a surprise [^5].
