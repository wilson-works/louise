# A quiet home office: overview

> An invented example from Louise's demo library. The sources are made up and live on example.org.

## Where the noise gets in

In a typical small room, gaps around the door let in more noise than the wall beside it [^1]. A door with a 1 cm
gap underneath can let through as much sound as a hole the size of a paperback [^1].

## Fixes that need no building work

- A self-adhesive seal around the door frame and a brush strip at the bottom of the door [^2].
- Heavy, lined curtains that reach past the window frame on every side [^3].
- A bookcase full of books against a shared wall.
- A thick rug on a hard floor, which mostly helps with echo rather than outside noise [^4].

## Echo on calls

Echo comes from hard, bare surfaces near the microphone, so soft things behind and beside you help most [^4]. A
headset microphone close to the mouth picks up far less of the room than a laptop microphone [^5].

## In short

Seal the door first, then the window, then soften the room for calls [^1] [^2] [^4].
